Scope: repository-wide visual system (tokens, primitives, chrome). Visitor mode: **Operate**.
Audience: a micro-seller running their shop one-handed on a mid-range Android, outdoors, in sun;
and the buyer who arrives from a shared link. Task: publish and manage goods; browse, ask, order.
Constraint: no desktop layouts, uncropped seller imagery, Spanish (es-MX), one webfont maximum.
Permissions granted this round: replace the visual world outright. The rotulación build on this
branch is the anti-reference, not a starting point.
Held: bottom-tab navigation shape, the sprout mark and the name, component APIs, copy, behavior,
and the colour **architecture** (one signature for the seller's own things, one for services, one
for the buy side, one for confirmed, ink for structure).

## Direction contract

**THESIS.** The shop is a card that gets stamped. Alkachof's catalog is capped at 25 items by the
server, so it *is* a tarjeta de abonos: a fixed grid of numbered boxes, some filled, some plainly
empty. Publishing fills a box; an order advances by taking another stamp. This refuses the
category default — white cards, soft elevation, one friendly accent — and it refuses the
incumbent rotulación build, whose paint was applied to surfaces rather than derived from what the
product actually counts.

**OWN-WORLD.** Manila card stock and stamp-pad ink. A warm mid-light card ground, receipt-paper
boards, warm graphite structure, and **violeta de sello** as the signature — the purple stamp ink
of Latin American small-business paperwork, which is neither the signal red nor the rosa this
product already tried. Añil for services, amarillo for the buy side, verde for confirmed, rojo de
sello for destructive. The world's real discipline is that it carries **three marking systems, not
one**: a printed rule (the card's own lithography), a stamp impression (an act someone performed),
and a punch or perforation (something spent or removed). Every state therefore has a non-colour
channel, which is the specific failure this product has hit twice. Recognizable with all content
removed by: the numbered box grid, the stamp impression sitting slightly off-register, and the
ruled fill-in line under every value.

**STORY.** The seller understands the app is the card they already keep in their wallet, and that
their shop has a countable number of boxes with some still empty. They believe filling one is a
small, finished act. They publish an item and share the link.

**FIRST VIEWPORT.** The catalog: the shop name at the top as a rubber-stamp impression, slightly
off-register and ink-starved at one edge, over a ruled card header carrying payment and delivery on
fill-in lines. Below, the goods as numbered boxes — each photograph at its own natural height, never
cropped, with its folio stamped in the corner and its name and price written on a ruled line
beneath. Empty boxes are drawn, numbered and plainly empty, because a shop with room left should
look like one. The primary action is a stamp.

**FORM.** Tarjeta de abonos (the installment/layaway card of informal Latin American credit),
position 1 of 7 on the ordered grounded list, taken as IMPECCABLE'S PICK over the assigned roll
(La Placa, index 6). Seed key 78975501, re-roll round 1.

**SIGNATURE INTERACTION.** The stamp: a control is pressed and leaves an impression — it lands
slightly off-register, ink-dense at the centre and starved at one edge, in a single 120ms step with
no fade. Motion is impression and punch, never dissolve. A disabled control flattens into the card
rather than fading, and a spent one is punched through.

**FINISH.** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
